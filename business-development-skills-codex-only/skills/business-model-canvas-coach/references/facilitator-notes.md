# Facilitator notes — first-draft Business Model Canvas

Support English and German with the same sequence and assessment standards. Student-facing language and controls follow language-and-course.md. These notes are not part of the student dialogue.

These are design notes for course staff, not compulsory student reading.

## What the skill tries to teach
- Articulate an existing idea before mapping it to a framework.
- Identify all nine blocks, in a repeatable order.
- Recognize plausible challenges and trade-offs without needing to perfect a hypothesis.
- Exercise choice over when to defend a decision and when to proceed.
- Notice dependencies between market, operations, and economics.

## Student-submitted evidence
If the course assesses the interaction protocol, request the **actual transcript** (including student messages), the final nine-block canvas, and perhaps a short reflection. The summary generated by the skill is not a substitute for the original dialogue; its wording may reflect AI assistance.

## Assessment caution
The number of `I don't think so!` choices, conversation length, and prompt sophistication are not reliable measures of learning. A student who judiciously chooses `NEXT` may demonstrate sound judgment. Consider evaluating conceptual coherence and the student's ability to explain decisions in their own words. An instructor—not the skill—determines grades.

## Suggested short pilot
1. Student provides a very clear idea, then uses `NEXT` for every block.
2. Student provides an ambiguous idea and defends twice on one block.
3. Student enters `NEXT` without any answer: the skill should record an unknown and advance.
4. Student argues against the AI's challenge without typing the word `I don't think so!`.
5. Student changes target customer at Step 7: earlier canvas should be updated without restart.
6. Student completes nine steps: skill should summarize, then point to Market & Marketing Coach and explain how to carry the summary forward.

## Canonical framework
The nine names are those of the Business Model Canvas by Osterwalder and Pigneur. The teaching order here is selected for continuity; the visual positions on the traditional canvas are different. Official description: https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas

## Question and counterargument balance
Use opening questions to elicit the student's idea; use a defensible counterargument to test a clear proposal. At Steps 2 and 5 invite students to identify their weakest assumption before offering your own analysis, unless they have already done so. Clarifications and these two self-assessment prompts do not carry buttons. An explicit request to proceed always advances to the next canvas block.

Check these cases when piloting:
- A vague proposal gets a clarification rather than an objection based on invented detail.
- At Step 2, the student gets the weakest-assumption prompt once; “I don't know” leads to feedback, while NEXT leads to Step 3.
- A student who already identifies a weakness is not asked to repeat it.
- A convincing defense is accepted rather than met with a new objection.
- A coherent proposal is recognised without forced disagreement; unresolved assumptions stay labelled.
- Final summaries distinguish weaknesses students identified independently from issues the coach introduced, without assigning grades.
